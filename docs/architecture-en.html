ALWA System Map

One WhatsApp number, ten services behind it. This page shows what each part does, who's connected to whom, and how a single message travels through the system.

1WhatsApp number — every flow runs through the same line
3routing layers: identity, intent, action

map

What's connected to what?

The server in the middle is where everything meets. The customer comes in from the left and gets the reply back on the left; in between, the server consults the model, writes data to the right, and alerts staff through the channels below when needed.

flow

What happens when a message arrives?

Every message passes through the same three gates. The key point: intent is measured fresh on every message — a complaint that appears in the middle of a pricing conversation is caught instantly.

services

What does each service actually do?

Every card lists that service's job and — the important part — what happens if it goes down. The system is deliberately built this way: no single service outage silences the bot completely.

data

Five tables, five questions

What lives where in Supabase, and who looks at each one day to day?

TableWhat it holdsWho looks at it
students Registered student and parent numbers — the list that lets the bot say "this is our customer." Loaded once via CSV, updated occasionally
conversations Every incoming and outgoing message, tagged with its intent. The monthly report is generated from this table. Automatic — nobody touches it by hand
leads People who want to enroll or be called back, with their last message and topic. Sales — updates status after calling
complaints Complaint text, response deadline, and status. An open record past its deadline triggers a reminder email. Branch manager — marks resolved once handled
survey_responses Satisfaction survey button answers, along with which survey they belong to. End-of-term review

example

"We weren't happy with the teacher last term"

This sentence isn't in any keyword list — yet the system still catches it as a complaint. Step by step, here's what happens:

01
The message arrives.

Meta drops it at the webhook address; the server picks it up within seconds.

02
Button or profanity check.

Neither — the flow continues.

03
The number is looked up.

If it's in the students table, support mode kicks in; otherwise sales mode.

04
The model is called.

A reply, an intent (complaint), and a confidence score (0.9) all come back in one shot.

05
An empathetic reply goes out.

A short message with no defensiveness and no sales talk.

06
A complaint record opens.

Written to the complaints table with a deadline 24 hours out.

07
Staff get an email.

With the customer's number, the complaint text, and the response deadline.

08
The customer gets a promise.

"Your complaint is recorded — we'll get back to you within 24 hours at the latest."

09
If the deadline passes, a reminder fires.

If the record is still open, the hourly check sends a second email — nothing gets forgotten.
